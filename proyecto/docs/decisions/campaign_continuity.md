# Campaign Continuity

Status: **APPROVED**

## Core rule

**The game does not replay Star Trek television episodes or films.**

Series and films define:
- the universe,
- historical context,
- institutions,
- characters,
- ships,
- technology,
- relationships,
- political state,
- culture,
- chronology up to the campaign starting point.

They do **not** define a mandatory future script for the player's campaign.

## Canonical starting state

When a campaign starts on date `D`, the game builds the world using canon facts established **up to D**.

Examples:
- Jean-Luc Picard may command USS Enterprise-D.
- Benjamin Sisko may command Deep Space 9.
- Kathryn Janeway may command USS Voyager.
- the correct uniforms, technology, political relations and known events exist for that date.

These are world-state facts, not instructions to replay episodes.

## Campaign divergence

From the moment the campaign begins, the live game state becomes authoritative.

Events that canonically happened after the campaign start date are **not guaranteed to happen**.

They may:
- never occur,
- occur differently,
- occur to different people,
- be prevented,
- emerge naturally because the simulation produces similar conditions,
- be used as optional inspiration by the AI Director.

They are never mandatory simply because they happened in a television episode or film.

## Example: Voyager

Choosing a Voyager-based campaign does **not** mean replaying seven seasons and inevitably ending with Voyager returning to the Alpha Quadrant.

Voyager, Janeway and her crew can exist as the canonical starting context, but the campaign becomes its own history once play begins.

If the campaign starts after a canonical event, that event is already part of the past. If it starts before it, the event is not destiny.

## Example: Enterprise-D

USS Enterprise-D and Jean-Luc Picard may exist in the same world as the player's officer.

This does **not** mean the player must replay TNG episodes, join Picard's story arcs or reproduce the Enterprise-D's canonical future.

Picard is an NPC/captain living in the same universe, not the protagonist whose television scripts control the game.

## Historical canon vs campaign history

The project therefore distinguishes:

```text
CANONICAL HISTORY BEFORE CAMPAIGN START
            ↓
      CAMPAIGN START
            ↓
EMERGENT PLAYER CAMPAIGN HISTORY
```

Future screen canon remains reference material, not a predetermined timeline.

## Save rule

Each save preserves its own campaign history.

Once a campaign diverges, later recaps, NPC memories, promotions, ship damage, assignments, deaths, discoveries and political consequences come from the save's actual history, not from television continuity.

## AI rule

The AI Director may use canon events as:
- inspiration,
- historical parallels,
- optional event seeds,
- background context.

It must never force the campaign to reproduce a known episode/film outcome merely to "stay canon".

## Design principle

**Canon defines the starting universe. The player creates the future.**
