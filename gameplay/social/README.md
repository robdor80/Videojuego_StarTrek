# Social Simulation

Status: **FOUNDATION v0.1 — IN PROGRESS**

## Purpose

Model persistent social life between:

- player ↔ NPC;
- NPC ↔ NPC.

The ship, Academy, station or colony must feel like a community that existed before the player arrived and continues to exist when the player is absent.

## Core rule

> **Relationship is multidimensional history, not a single friendship meter.**

The system must represent compatible combinations such as:

- high professional respect + low personal affinity;
- close friendship + weak professional confidence;
- strong mentorship + no friendship;
- attraction without trust;
- trust without romance;
- conflict without permanent hatred.

## Relationship dimensions

The foundation supports independent dimensions such as:

- familiarity;
- personal affinity;
- personal trust;
- professional respect;
- command confidence;
- friendship;
- mentorship;
- shared history;
- conflict state;
- emotional intimacy;
- romantic interest;
- sexual attraction/interest;
- relationship state;
- boundaries/consent;
- privacy expectations.

Not every relationship needs every dimension.

## No global karma

There is no universal social score.

A character may be:

- admired professionally by Engineering;
- disliked personally by one officer;
- trusted by Command;
- unknown to most civilians;
- romantically involved with one NPC.

These states coexist.

## Knowledge and social truth

Relationships do not create omniscience.

NPC knowledge of another character still depends on:

- direct interaction;
- observation;
- disclosure;
- reports;
- rumor or other valid information paths.

Private relationships are not automatically known ship-wide.

## Familiarity

Repeated plausible encounters may create familiarity.

Familiarity is not friendship.

A crewmember seen every morning in Ten Forward may become recognizable without ever becoming a close friend.

## Friendship

Friendship emerges from compatible shared history, voluntary interaction, trust, affinity and continuity.

Proximity alone is insufficient.

## Professional relationships

Professional trust and respect are distinct from personal liking.

These dimensions may influence:

- willingness to rely on an officer;
- mentorship;
- recommendations;
- invitations to additional responsibility;
- professional debrief tone.

They do not bypass qualifications, authority or formal career rules.

## Conflict and reconciliation

Conflict preserves history.

An apology may be:

- accepted;
- partially accepted;
- rejected;
- accepted without restoring trust.

Reconciliation does not erase the original event.

## Adult romantic and sexual relationships

Romantic and sexual mechanics apply only to adults.

The model distinguishes:

- attraction;
- romantic interest;
- emotional intimacy;
- sexual intimacy;
- established relationship;
- exclusivity/relationship expectations;
- boundaries;
- consent.

Rules:

- attraction does not imply romance;
- romance does not imply sexual consent;
- established relationship does not imply automatic consent;
- friendship/favors/rank do not imply consent;
- consent is contextual and may be refused or withdrawn;
- intimacy is part of character life, not a consumable stat reward.

## NPC autonomy

NPCs may develop relationships with one another without player involvement.

Possible outcomes include:

- friendship;
- rivalry;
- mentorship;
- dating;
- established partnership;
- separation;
- reconciliation;
- remaining single;
- no attraction.

## Social circles

Characters may belong to overlapping circles:

- department;
- duty shift;
- Academy cohort;
- junior officers;
- senior staff;
- poker group;
- medical team;
- training group.

Circle membership does not mean every member is friends with every other member.

## AI boundary

AI may express an existing relationship state and interpret social context.

AI may not:

- invent shared history;
- invent attraction/relationship state retroactively;
- override consent;
- grant professional authority;
- expose private knowledge without a valid information path.

## CoreRPG boundary

Generic relationship graphs, memories, knowledge provenance and social-state persistence are candidates for CoreRPG.

Star Trek defines the universe-specific professional, rank, duty and Starfleet context layered on top.
